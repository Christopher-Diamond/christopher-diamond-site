Vidyāpati (c. 1370–1450) was a poet and scholar at the royal court of Mithila, in present-day northern Bihar. He wrote in three languages: Sanskrit, Avahaṭṭha (a late form of Apabhraṃśa), and Maithili, the vernacular of Mithila. This project translates two of his works into English.

        <h4>Padāvalī</h4>
        <p>Vidyāpati's short songs (<em>padas</em>) in Maithili, on love and devotion, were collected in anthologies known as <em>padāvalī</em>. They were sung far beyond Mithila, at the Malla courts of Nepal and among Vaiṣṇava communities in Bengal. I am preparing a new edition and translation from the oldest surviving manuscripts.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="col-sm mt-3 mt-md-0">
        <h4>Kīrttilatā</h4>
        <p>The <em>Kīrttilatā</em> ("Creeper of Fame") is a chronicle in Avahaṭṭha verse and prose honouring Vidyāpati's patron, Kīrttisiṃha, and his efforts to recover his throne. It includes a vivid description of the city of Jaunpur. I am preparing an annotated English translation.</p>
