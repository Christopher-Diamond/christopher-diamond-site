I am Senior Lecturer in Hindi-Urdu and South Asian Studies at the Australian National University, where I convene the Hindi-Urdu, South Asian Studies and Tibetan programs. My research examines the literature, music and manuscript cultures of premodern North and East India.

Much of this work concerns Mithila, a region in northern Bihar and southern Nepal, and its language, Maithili. At its centre are the lyric poems (*padas*) of Vidyāpati (c. 1370–1450), a poet of the Mithila court whose songs were later adopted by the Malla kings of Nepal and by Vaiṣṇava devotional communities in Bengal. A new edition and translation of the oldest manuscripts of these songs is currently in preparation, together with a study of how kings and brahmins in Nepal and Bihar used them to project power and prestige.

After training in Hindi and music at SOAS, University of London, I completed my MA and PhD in South Asian Languages and Literature at the University of Washington. Fieldwork and archival research have taken me to Bihar, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh and Nepal for more than a decade. My sources are in Maithili, Hindi (including Braj Bhāṣā and Avadhī), Bengali, Urdu, Sanskrit, Persian and Apabhraṃśa, and my other research interests include *bhakti* literature, performance, and eastern Indian versions of the epics and Purāṇas.

Alongside teaching and research, I co-founded [ANUBhasha](https://anubhasha.org), a collective for the digitisation and digital repatriation of premodern South Asian texts, and co-chair the [Mithila Studies Network](https://mithilastudies.org). Public writing on South Asian languages and literature is another part of this work.
